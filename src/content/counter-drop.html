01

Why I built it

At every print and xerox shop near my college, the routine is the same: you message your file to the owner's personal WhatsApp, stand around asking whether it's done, and your Aadhaar scan stays on a stranger's phone indefinitely.

Counter Drop replaces that with a QR code on the counter. You scan it, upload from your phone's browser, see the price and wait before you send, and get a token like A-07. Staff work from a live queue board. Ten minutes after you collect, your files are deleted and you get a deletion receipt.

The token is the hero. Everything else gets out of its way.

02

The architecture

On AWS, nothing runs between requests. CloudFront serves the PWA, API Gateway sends every request to Lambda, and DynamoDB is the single source of truth. Four Lambda functions come from one Go binary, which picks its role from the CD_RUNTIME environment variable.

03, the hard part

A live queue with no server to remember it

A queue sounds like a list in memory. On Lambda there is no memory between requests, so every piece of state has to live in DynamoDB, be readable in one cheap query, and change safely when two staff members tap at once. I designed the table around the questions the app asks, not around entities.

Question the app asksKeyHow it's answered
What's on the shop board?GSI1 S#shop#OPENQueued, printing and ready jobs, sorted by when they joined. One query.
What was done today?GSI2 S#shop#D#dayEvery submitted job for one business day.
What's the next token?TOK#day#laneAn atomic ADD on a counter per shop, day and lane. Two taps can't get the same number.
Which files are due for deletion?GSI4 DUEJobs sorted by their next deletion time; the sweeper reads only what's due.
Is this name or link already taken?SLUG# and NAME#Guard items written with a condition, so uniqueness holds without a transaction table.

Indexes are sparseAn item only appears in the index when it has that index's key, so finished jobs drop out on their own.: a job leaves the board index the moment it is collected, so the board query never grows with history.

04

A job, start to finish

1
Customer

Scans the shop's QR poster and uploads from the phone browser. Sees price and wait time before sending.

2
APItoDynamoDB

Atomically takes the next token for that lane and day, say A-07, and adds the job to the board.

3
StafftoAPI

Claims the job, prints, marks it ready. Every change is an event on the job's timeline.

4
Staff

Marks it collected with cash or UPI. Undo and cancel-with-reason are always one tap away.

5
SweepertoS3

Ten minutes after pickup, deletes the files and records it; the customer's phone shows a deletion receipt.

05

Decisions and what they cost

I choseOverBecauseIt costs
One DynamoDB tableA table per entityEvery screen is one query; pay per request at shop scale.Access patterns must be designed up front.
Lambda behind API GatewayAn always-on containerA shop is idle most of the day; idle should cost nothing.Cold starts and a 15-minute limit per run.
One Go binary, four rolesFour codebasesShared domain code and tests; the same code runs locally.A slightly larger binary per function.
Stream-driven pushesPushing from the APIWrites stay fast and every change is broadcast once.A second or so of stream delay.
Delete after pickupKeeping filesCustomers' ID scans shouldn't outlive the job.No reprints after ten minutes.

06

What I'd change next

Prepay with UPI

Planned for the next release: pay while uploading, so the counter only hands over prints.

A Windows print agent

Send jobs straight to the shop's printer instead of downloading them at the desk.

Phone OTP and self-signup

Let a new shop set itself up without me creating its first staff link.

Load-test the board

Measure stream-to-phone delay at rush hour before a second shop joins.
